Garden Design Basics for Small Spaces

By TruLeaf Landscapes  ·  4 min read

Small gardens can be just as impactful as large ones when designed thoughtfully. In Kangaroo Valley and the Shoalhaven, where properties often have limited outdoor space, smart design maximises every square metre. Here is how to create a beautiful, functional small garden.

Maximise Vertical Space

Use vertical elements — climbing plants on trellises, wall-mounted planters, and tiered structures — to draw the eye upward and create the illusion of more space. This technique works particularly well in small urban and suburban gardens.

Create Zoned Areas

Divide your small garden into distinct zones — a dining area, a lounge space, a planting bed, and a mulch or path area. This creates visual interest and makes the space feel larger. Use different textures, heights, and colours to differentiate each zone.

Lighting Can Dramatically Change the Feel

Lighting can dramatically change the feel of a small garden. Use warm, layered lighting to highlight key features, extend the usable hours of the space, and create a sense of depth.

Choose the Right Plants

Select plants that thrive in your specific conditions — light, soil, and moisture. Use a mix of heights, textures, and colours to create depth and interest. Evergreens provide year-round structure, while seasonal plants add colour and interest.

Invest in Quality Elements

In a small space, the few elements you choose should be high quality. A good fence, a well-made path, and quality lighting will make the garden feel complete and well-maintained.
