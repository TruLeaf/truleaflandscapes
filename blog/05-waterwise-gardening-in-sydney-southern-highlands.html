Waterwise Gardening in Sydney Southern Highlands

By TruLeaf Landscapes  ·  4 min read

Waterwise gardening is about designing and maintaining a garden that thrives with minimal watering. In Kangaroo Valley and the Shoalhaven, where summer droughts are common and water restrictions may apply, waterwise gardening is both practical and sustainable. Here is how to create a garden that conserves water and stays healthy.

1. Choose Drought-Tolerant Plants

Select plants that are naturally adapted to dry conditions. Native species like Westringia, Banksia, and Callistemon are excellent choices. Exotic options like Lavandula and Rosemary also thrive in dry conditions. Group plants with similar water needs together.

2. Mulch Heavily

A thick layer of mulch (5–8 cm) around plants reduces evaporation, suppresses weeds, and keeps soil temperatures stable. Organic mulches like bark chips and compost also improve soil structure and water retention.

3. Install Efficient Irrigation

Drip irrigation delivers water directly to plant roots, minimising evaporation and runoff. Use a smart controller that adjusts watering based on weather conditions. Water deeply but infrequently to encourage deep root growth.

4. Improve Soil Quality

Healthy soil holds water better. Add organic matter like compost to improve soil structure and water-holding capacity. Avoid compacting soil, which reduces water infiltration.
