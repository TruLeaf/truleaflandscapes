Local character

Here's what makes this part of the Shoalhaven distinct — and how it affects your garden.

Energy

The riverfront is exposed to southerly swell in summer, and the low-lying flats are prone to heavy rain and minor flooding after a big rain event. The ridge tops catch drying westerly winds in cold fronts, and the flat ground can stay waterlogged in winter. Aspect matters: a site on the river flats is sheltered but may flood; a site on the ridge is windier but better drained. The summer thunderstorms that move through the district can bring sudden heavy rain and flash flooding.

Soil & planting

Bomaderry sits on alluvial flats over gravel, with deeper, moisture-retentive soils near the river and free-draining sandy loams on the higher ground. The alluvial sites are great for gardening but can be waterlogged in winter; the higher sites drain quickly and need mulching and organic matter in summer. Match the plant to the soil you have — the river flats suit moisture-loving plants; the ridges suit drought-tolerant species.

Landmarks

The Shoalhaven River, the historic Bomaderry Bridge, and the township's riverside reserve and parklands. The surrounding farmland and forest frame the district's character as a river-side gateway to the South Coast.

Local questions

Common questions from bomaderry properties — answered for this part of the district.

Will the summer wind ruin a new hedge?
Yes, on an exposed ridge or a flat site that dries out in a westerly. Plant on a sheltered aspect, water deeply in the first summer, and mulch well. A windbreak on the windward side gives new plantings the best start.
What soil do I need for native gardens here?
Bomaderry's alluvial soils are rich and deep, good for most garden plants if drainage is good. The higher free-draining sites need organic matter and mulch. Match the plant to the soil you have — the river flats suit moisture-loving plants; the ridges suit drought-tolerant species.
When is the best planting window?
Autumn is ideal: warm soil, incoming rain, and established root growth before the winter and summer extremes. Spring planting works, provided you water through the first dry spell.
