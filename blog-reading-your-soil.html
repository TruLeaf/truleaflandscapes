Why the soil matters more than the plants

1 October 2026

People usually come to us with a plant problem — something's not growing, something's died, something looks sick — and more often than not the plant was never really the issue. The soil underneath it was. Kangaroo Valley and the surrounding Shoalhaven country isn't one soil type, it changes block by block, and a plant that thrives on one side of a property can struggle twenty metres away if the ground underneath is different.

What we're actually looking at

Before we plant anything, we dig a hole and have a look — not a lab test, just a spade and an honest assessment of what's there. Sandy loam over sandstone drains fast and dries out fast, so it needs mulch and organic matter or it won't hold moisture through summer. Clay-heavy ground near the river flats holds water well but can sit wet after heavy rain, which rots roots on plants that need good drainage. Neither is bad soil, they're just different problems, and the fix for one can make the other worse.

Matching the plant to what's actually there

The easiest mistake to make is picking a plant because it looks right in a photo, rather than because it suits the ground it's going into. We'd rather talk a client out of a plant that's going to struggle than put it in and watch it fail — that's not a great outcome for anyone, and it usually means doing the job twice. A native that's adapted to free-draining sandstone soil isn't going to be happy in heavy clay that stays wet all winter, no matter how well it's planted.

Why this saves money and disappointment

Getting the soil right before you plant is cheaper than replacing dead plants six months later, and it's a lot less frustrating. If a garden bed has been struggling for a while, the plant list usually isn't the first thing we look at — the soil is.
