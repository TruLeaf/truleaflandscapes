Hedging and pruning: getting the timing right

5 November 2026

A hedge cut at the wrong time of year doesn't just look rough for a week — depending on the species and how hard you go, it can set the plant back a full growing season, and on a formal hedge that shows for a long time. Most of the mistakes we're called in to fix come down to timing rather than technique: someone's cut hard in the middle of a heatwave, or right before a frost, and the plant's had no window to recover before the next stress hits it.

Light trims versus a hard reduction

A light shape-up to keep lines clean can happen through most of the growing season without much risk, because you're only removing the new growth on the outside of the plant. A hard reduction — cutting back into old wood to bring a hedge down in height or width — is a different job entirely. That needs to happen when the plant has the whole season ahead of it to put on new growth and recover, which in this district generally means late winter into early spring, before the heat sets in and before the plant's put its energy into flowering or fruiting.

Why it's different here

Kangaroo Valley gets real frost in the low-lying pockets through winter, and a hard-pruned plant with fresh, soft growth is more vulnerable to frost damage than one left alone. On the flip side, a plant that's been reduced right before a dry, hot stretch in summer is pushing out new leaves with no rain behind it, which stresses the root system at the same time it's trying to recover from the cut. We watch both ends of that — the frost risk in winter and the heat and dry risk in summer — before we decide when to go in on a hard prune.

What we tell clients honestly

If a hedge badly needs a hard reduction and the timing isn't right, we'll say so rather than do it anyway because it's convenient. Sometimes that means a light tidy now and the real cut-back scheduled for a few months later. It's not the answer people always want to hear, but it's the one that gets the hedge back to looking right instead of looking patchy and stressed for the next year.
