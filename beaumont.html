Local character

Here's what makes this part of the Shoalhaven distinct — and how it affects your garden.

Energy

Beaumont sits in the rain shadow of the inland ranges, which means it is drier and sunnier than the coastal strip. Winter frosts are common in low-lying pockets and the grassy flats; the exposed ridge tops catch the driving southerlies. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are heavier here than on the coast, and the hot dry north-Westerlies in January and February can parch a freshly planted garden. Aspect matters just as much here as anywhere: a south-facing slope stays damp and slow to warm; a north-facing one bakes in summer.

Soil & planting

The village and its immediate surrounds sit on formed brown soils over clay, which are workable and hold moisture reasonably well for grazing and native planting. Around the creeks and on the lower slopes the alluvial soils are deeper and richer. The higher country tends to be sandy and free-draining, which means it needs regular watering and mulching for anything other than drought-adapted species.

Landmarks

The heritage-listed Beaumont School of Arts, the village's old orchard blocks, and the patchwork of pine plantations and grassland that characterises the western edge of the valley. The road toward Braidwood cuts through the district's dairy and grazing country.

Local questions

Common questions from beaumont properties — answered for this part of the district.

Will the winter wind ruin a new hedge?
It can, on the exposed ridges. Plant in a sheltered position, stake for two winters, and choose a hardy species that suits the local soil. Low-lying frost pockets also need protection — a thick mulch and windbreak give new plantings the best start.
What soil do I need for native gardens here?
Beaumont's brown soils over clay are workable for most natives if drainage is good. The sandy higher country needs extra organic matter and mulch to hold moisture. Match the plant to the soil — it is the difference between a garden that thrives and one that struggles.
When is the best planting window?
Autumn is ideal here: the soil is still warm, rain is on the way, and the plants establish before the winter frosts and the summer heat. Spring planting works too, provided you water through the first dry spell.
